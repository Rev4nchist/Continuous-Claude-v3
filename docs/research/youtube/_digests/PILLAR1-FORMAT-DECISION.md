# Pillar 1 Presentation Format — Decision

## 1. DECISION

**Winner: Design 2, Graded Decision Cards (D/R/A), as the base structure — modified by four grafts that are load-bearing, not decorative.**

Totals across the three lenses:

| Design | VP | CTO | Operator | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 — Graded Decision Cards | 7 | 7 | 8 | **22** |
| 4 — Research Docket | 9 | 5 | 7 | 21 |
| 1 — Basis-Gated Ledger | 5 | 9 | 6 | 20 |
| 3 — Thesis on Trial | 8 | 8 | 4 | 20 |

**The sentence that decides it:** Design 2 is the only format no lens scored below 7, and its two headline defects (no ask, apparatus above payload) are fixed by moving text, while the higher-peaking designs each carry a defect that cannot be moved — Design 4 has no place to put a disagreement and marks a measured result and a slide identically, Design 1 makes the operator write every claim twice, Design 3 requires an undelegable weekly act of thesis-building.

**How the lenses were resolved rather than averaged.** The real axis was not brevity against evidence — it was *where the sponsor sentence sits* against *whether the evidence apparatus costs anything*. Design 2 is the only design whose honesty apparatus is free: the grades ride inside the evidence rows the card already needs, so the header tally is derived, not written, and there is no second copy of the section in a trace table. That resolves the operator lens outright. The VP lens then costs one structural change (a ≤25-word sponsor line as the mandatory first line of every card, and a Fourth artifact in the heading slot instead of a card number) and one reordering (synthesis first, tally on one line). The CTO lens costs one conditional block and one tightened grade definition. Both fit. The reverse construction — Design 4 or Design 1 as base — needed three or four patches each, two of which the authors admitted they had not tested.

**What we are trading away, named:**

- **Argued synthesis.** We get 70 words of read with a falsifier, not Design 3's developed thesis. A week where the corpus genuinely contains a strategic reframe will be under-served, and the escape hatch is an ESCALATE, which is a question, not an argument.
- **A dedicated audit appendix.** Evidence is inline only. Christian can verify any single claim to the second, but he cannot scan one table to see the grade distribution beyond the header tally. This is a deliberate cost: the trace table was the single most expensive recurring item in the comparison and it duplicated the section.
- **A priced cost estimate.** Design 4's "~2 days" is demoted to a fixed five-term size band. We lose apparent precision to stop a fabricated number wearing citation-shaped authority in the entry that asks for a decision.
- **Talk-level coverage.** No talk ever gets a heading, a stance label, or a summary. A reader cannot tell from the section which talks were watched, only how many, and what came out.

## 2. GRAFTS

**Taken, with the reason each earns its place:**

1. **The ≤25-word sponsor line as the mandatory first line of every card (Design 4).** The highest-value single rule in all four designs, per the VP lens. It costs nothing — it replaces Design 2's `Benefit:` line rather than adding to it.
2. **Workstream → artifact in the heading slot (Design 4).** Design 2 kept talks out of headings by rule; this does it by structure, which holds at 11pm on a Friday when a rule does not. It also gives the VP the portfolio index he reads by.
3. **The one-line badge honesty note (Design 4).** Resolves in one sentence the badge-semantics strain Design 1 spends a self-declared weakness on, and lets us drop the badge column from the ledger, which was near information-free at four-of-five NEXT.
4. **THE READ, 70 words, with confidence, a falsifier, and one line of self-dissent (Design 3).** This is the synthesis gap the VP named as Design 4's fatal flaw and Design 2 does not have either. Capped hard at 70 words plus two labelled lines, so it cannot grow into an essay. The self-dissent line replaces Design 3's separate 90-word dissent block — same function, a fifth of the space.
5. **The counter line, including "New build asked for / New spend" (Design 3).** One line, answers the VP's first question before he asks it, and forces the denominator framing on talk count.
6. **The conditional Contested block, six labelled lines including "what is not contested" and "the decision that holds either way" (Design 1).** The CTO lens treats this as a gate, not a deduction: without it the format's default output is convergence. Kept strictly conditional, which is the right frequency — Design 3's mistake was making disagreement the architecture and paying for it every week.
7. **The cadence / carry-forward rule (Design 1).** All three judges asked for it and it closes Design 2's only structural rot vector. Extended with the enforcement Design 1 admits it lacks: a PILOT may be carried as in-progress for at most two checkpoints.
8. **The discard line and the killed-own-inference clause (Design 1), upgraded by the "never evidence" standing list (Design 3).** Design 1 caught the view-count trap by luck; the standing list makes it a rule. Two lines a week, and the most trust-earning content in any of the four examples.
9. **The ASR rule with teeth (Design 3):** a HIGH-risk mangle on a number-bearing claim is excluded and the exclusion is stated. The only mechanism among the four that removes a claim on the record instead of caveating it.
10. **The Forced-Fit Test plus a shelf with a promotion trigger and a named watcher (Design 4).** Design 2 has no visible overflow at all. The watcher is the patch its own author says it needs.
11. **The extended activity-verb banlist (Design 4):** explored, looked at, dove into, interesting. It is a grep.
12. **Tightened D (from the CTO's A·built / A·view critique).** D now requires that the audience *saw* a result, figure, artifact or running system, with a mandatory sample-size bracket. A mechanism drawn on slides is R. This is the single most important graft: it closes Design 1's over-wide DEMO bucket and Design 4's soft-edged ◆ in one definition change, without adding a fourth grade.

**Rejected, and why:**

- **Design 1's separate evidence-trace table.** Every claim written twice, and in its own worked example the trace was longer than the section. Inline citation with a derived header tally gets the same auditability for a fraction of the weekly cost.
- **Design 3's per-talk stance strips.** They reinstate talk titles as headings, which is the exact failure the format exists to prevent, and one strip in the worked example spent a phone screen on a talk whose bearing was "nothing current."
- **Design 4's priced "Cost to act."** A weekly invented number with citation-shaped authority, load-bearing in the escalate entry. Replaced by a fixed five-term band.
- **Design 4's ◆ ◐ ◇ glyphs.** Near-indistinguishable on a phone and they conflate a measurement with a slide. Letters carry more information for less effort.
- **Design 1's three-row minimum on the ledger.** A floor is an instruction to manufacture decisions in a thin week. Replaced with an explicit zero-is-a-valid-answer rule.
- **Design 3's separate 90-word dissent block.** Function kept as one line inside THE READ. A second reader or a cross-model pass would do this better than either version; that is a process fix, not a format fix.
- **Design 1's printed-every-week basis-gate paragraph on screen two.** The gate is real and stays, but it lives in the standing footer below where the VP stops reading, not above the payload.
- **A per-card So-What Ladder.** It duplicates the sponsor line almost word for word. One ladder-equivalent per section: THE READ is rung 1–2, each card's sponsor line is rung 3.

## 3. THE SPEC

Hand this to a subagent as a contract. Every field is mandatory unless marked optional. A card missing any mandatory field is invalid and does not ship.

### Section order and headings (exact)

```
## Pillar 1 — Research → Decisions
### The read
### The docket            (ledger table)
### Carried forward       (omit only in week 1, replaced by the standing sentence)
### Decisions             (the cards)
### Contested: <question> (conditional — only when sources genuinely conflict)
### Set aside, shelved, confirmed
### Not counted
### Standing notes        (footer: grade key, gate, never-evidence, ASR)
```

### Length budget

- **Hard cap 1,400 words including the standing footer. Target 1,000.**
- THE READ: 70 words plus three labelled lines. Counter line: 1 line. Ledger: ≤6 rows. Cards: 90–130 words each, **cap 6, target 4**. Contested: ≤150 words. Set aside/shelf/confirmed: ≤200 words. Not counted: ≤80 words. Standing footer: ~140 words, unchanged week to week.
- **First thing to cut when over:** the ledger's "What changes" column (the sponsor lines carry it), then the sixth card, then the Contested block.

### THE READ — fixed shape

```
**The read.** <one claim about where the field is moving, ≤70 words, naming the
Fourth artifact it implicates>
`Confidence:` low | medium | high — <reason, ≤6 words>
`Would change this read if:` <a checkable condition>
`Strongest case against it:` <1 sentence, built only from this corpus>
```

If no checkable condition can be written, the read is not a claim; downgrade it to a question and say so.

### Counter line — fixed template

```
N talks ingested, M reviewed at depth (denominator, not progress) → K decisions:
a adopt · b pilot · c escalate · d watch · e set aside.
New build asked for: <X or none>. New spend: <Y or none>.
Evidence rows: n D · n R · n A.
```

The evidence tally counts card rows only, and is derived by counting them. **A week with zero decisions reports zero and says why.** There is no minimum.

### Badge honesty note (standing, one line, immediately after the counter line)

> Every decision below is NEXT. Research produces decisions; a badge upgrades when the change is in a connector, the harness, or the portal.

If a week genuinely produces a BUILT item off research, that line changes and names which.

### The docket — ledger table, ≤6 rows, 5 columns

`# | Workstream → artifact | Verdict | What changes at Fourth | Basis`

- **Verdict** is exactly one of: ADOPT · PILOT · SET ASIDE · WATCH · ESCALATE. A row that cannot start with one of those five is not a decision.
- **What changes at Fourth** is the card's sponsor line, compressed to ≤12 words.
- **Basis** is the card's strongest evidence grade with its bracket, e.g. `D (1 case)`.
- **Ordering:** any ESCALATE is hoisted to the top, because it is the only row that needs someone else's decision. Everything below it is ordered by workstream in delivery order — live production work, then next-up connectors, then the portal, then internal harness. Never by talk. Never by how interesting the finding was. **Never by cost.**

### Decision card — fixed 6 fields, exact order

```
**D-n · <workstream> → <named artifact> · <VERDICT> · NEXT · confirms|extends|contradicts**

<Sponsor line: ≤25 words, plain English, what it makes possible first, mechanism
second. The only line Carly must read.>

`Change:` <what we would actually do, one sentence.>
`Evidence:` 2–5 rows. Each row: <GRADE><bracket if D> <paraphrase> [vid:ID @M:SS]
`Weakest link:` <the softest specific thing this decision rests on. One line.>
`Wrong if:` <the falsifier — the condition under which this is a bad call.>
`Cost:` <band> · owner <name> · <decision owner, for ESCALATE only> · checkpoint <date>
```

- **Cost band is one of exactly five terms:** `hours` · `under a day` · `days` · `about a sprint` · `unpriced`. No other figure. No invented day counts.
- **Forced-Fit Test:** a card is valid only if the heading names a *specific artifact* (not a workstream) **and** `Wrong if` states a condition that could actually obtain. If either is vacuous, it is not a decision — it goes to the shelf with a trigger, or to Not counted.
- **Triviality screen:** if the change is a chore under an hour that we would have done anyway, it is not a card. Fold it into another card's `Change` line or drop it. The format must not pay rent in pennies.

### Citation contract

- Inline only, `[vid:ID @M:SS]`, on every evidence row. Multiple timestamps allowed in one row when a claim spans positions.
- **No evidence row, no claim.** A sentence anywhere in the section with no row behind it is deleted before it ships.
- **No separate trace table.** The header tally is the section-level view.
- Timestamps are second-accurate against the local corpus and must be re-checked, not remembered.

### Evidence strength — three grades, one glyph

- **`D` demonstrated** — the audience *saw* a result, figure, artifact, public issue trail, or running system. **A mandatory bracket states the size:** `D (1 case)`, `D (n=200)`, `D (n=2,000 skills, 1,500 issues)`. **A D row without a bracket is invalid** — do not delete it for space. A mechanism drawn on slides, or a team narrating an architecture, is **R, not D**.
- **`R` reported** — the speaker describes what their team runs, or a mechanism on slides, or self-reported platform telemetry. Nothing measured in front of the audience.
- **`A` asserted** — opinion, prediction, framing, taxonomy, or product roadmap.
- **`ⓥ`** appended to the grade when the speaker's employer sells the thing in that row.

### The gate (printed in the footer, enforced in drafting)

- **ADOPT** requires one `D`, or two independent `R` with at least one non-vendor, **plus** reversibility inside a week or in-week self-verification.
- **PILOT** requires at least one `D` or `R`, with stated kill criteria in `Wrong if`.
- **An A-only finding caps at WATCH.** However well argued, framing does not become a recommendation.
- **ESCALATE** keys off authority — does this change scope, timeline, risk, or spend on committed work — not evidence strength. It may carry any basis provided the basis is stated. This is how a well-argued opinion still reaches the sponsor, as an explicit question.
- **A `ⓥ` row never carries a decision alone.** It needs a second non-commercial row or the finding becomes a set-aside.
- **Never upgrade a grade to make a card look stronger. Downgrade the verdict instead** (adopt → pilot → watch).

### ASR rules

- All transcripts are auto-captions. **Proper nouns and product names come from each talk's published description, never from the captions.** Technical terms are paraphrased, never quoted.
- Flag inline as `⚠asr` only when the mangle is material to the claim.
- **A number-bearing claim whose ASR risk is high is excluded and the exclusion is stated in Not counted.** Not caveated — excluded.
- The footer carries one instructive instance per week, so the reader can calibrate rather than discount the whole section.

### Never evidence, ever

View counts, likes, applause, audience hand-raises, how many talks covered a theme, speaker seniority, speaker's employer. Popularity is not a grade.

### Relevance test (applied before a finding is drafted)

Does it bear on the Salesforce connector, the shared connector chassis, the NetSuite or Zendesk build, the developer portal, or our own harness and eval practice? **Interesting fails. Well-known fails.** A finding that fails goes to Not counted as part of the count, not as a titled line.

### Carry-forward rule

Every ADOPT, PILOT and ESCALATE reappears in the next week's **Carried forward** block with its checkpoint outcome, in one line each: `D-n <verb> <artifact> — done | slipped, why | resolved as X`. A WATCH carries an expiry date. **A PILOT may be carried as in-progress for at most two checkpoints, then it resolves or is set aside with a reason.** A decision cannot quietly evaporate and a backlog cannot read as output.

### Shelf rule

A finding that matters but maps to no current artifact gets one line, plus a **trigger** that promotes it to a card, plus a **named watcher and a review point**. No trigger, no watcher: it is dropped, and the drop is stated.

### Overflow rule (the 20-talk week)

Cap is 6 cards. Overflow is not silently dropped and not deferred indefinitely: it goes in a **Held at the cap** line — verb, artifact, citation, one clause each — and is either carded next week or dropped with a reason. Ranking for the cap: ESCALATE first, then by evidence grade, then by workstream delivery order. **Not by cost** — cheapest-first systematically promotes tweaks over structural findings.

### Banlist

No leverage, robust, seamless, unlock as a verb, paradigm. No best, optimal, always, never, guaranteed. Additionally banned in this section: **explored, looked at, dove into, interesting** — four activity verbs that let an entry dodge a verdict. Prefer: may, could, typically, the demo showed, data indicates.

### Degradation rule — the one-finding week

Keep: the section heading, THE READ (still 70 words, still with a falsifier), the counter line, the badge note, **Carried forward**, one card, Not counted, the standing footer. **Drop:** the ledger table (a one-row table is noise — the card is the ledger), Contested, Set aside if there is nothing to set aside. Total: about 350 words.

**The zero-finding week is legal and is not padded.** THE READ becomes a question rather than a claim, the counter line reads `→ 0 decisions`, Carried forward does the work, and Not counted says what was reviewed and what test it failed. A week that produces no decision reports no decision. That is the whole point of counting decisions.

### Standing notes

**Grades.** `D` demonstrated: shown or counted on stage, with the size in brackets; one shown case establishes that a failure mode exists, never its rate. `R` reported: the speaker describes what their team runs, or a mechanism on slides; nothing measured in front of the audience. `A` asserted: opinion, prediction, framing, or roadmap. `ⓥ` the speaker's employer sells the thing in that row, and a `ⓥ` row never carries a decision alone.
**Gate.** ADOPT needs one D, or two independent R with at least one non-vendor, plus reversibility inside a week. PILOT needs one D or R plus stated kill criteria. An A-only finding caps at WATCH. ESCALATE keys off authority, not evidence strength, provided the basis is stated.
**Never evidence.** View counts, likes, applause, hand-raises, how many talks covered a theme, speaker seniority, speaker's employer.
**ASR.** All transcripts are auto-captions. Names and product spellings come from each talk's published description, not the captions; technical terms are paraphrased, never quoted. ## 5. WHAT THIS FORMAT REFUSES TO DO

**Structurally prevented — the format itself blocks these:**

- **A reading list.** No talk can occupy a heading, a stance label, or a summary slot. Talk IDs appear only inside evidence rows. There is nothing to skim that shows what was watched.
- **Counting activity as output.** The only countable nouns are decisions, set-asides, shelf items and evidence rows. Talks-reviewed appears once, labelled a denominator, and the discard count appears with no titles.
- **A well-argued opinion becoming a recommendation.** An A-only finding caps at WATCH. The route to the sponsor for a persuasive frame is ESCALATE, which is explicitly a question.
- **A slide reading as a measurement.** Narrated architecture and mechanisms on slides are R. D requires the audience to have seen a result, and a D row without a sample-size bracket is invalid.
- **A vendor pitch carrying a decision.** `ⓥ` plus the second-row rule. D-6 shows the working: the check gets a pilot on our own logs, the product is set aside.
- **A claim with no citation.** No evidence row, no claim, enforced at draft time.
- **A high-risk ASR number surviving.** It is excluded and the exclusion is stated, so the cost is visible.
- **A decision quietly evaporating.** Carry-forward is mandatory, PILOT has a two-checkpoint ceiling, WATCH has an expiry.
- **Manufacturing decisions in a thin week.** No minimum row count, and the degradation rule makes a one-card or zero-card week a legal, unpadded output.
- **A vague finding filed as a decision.** The Forced-Fit Test requires a named artifact and a falsifier that could obtain.
- **Averaging two speakers who disagree.** The Contested block requires both positions with citations, plus what is *not* contested.
- **Popularity as evidence.** The standing list forecloses it, and the killed-inference line shows the temptation was real.

**Not prevented — watch these manually every week:**

- **A single grader with no audit.** Every D/R/A call, every ASR risk rating, and every relevance judgement is one person's, unrecorded. A second reader or a cross-model pass would catch drift; nothing in the format requires one. **This is the largest residual risk.**
- **Triviality that passes the Forced-Fit Test.** The screen catches vagueness. A real-but-tiny internal chore, correctly cited and falsifiable, still gets a card. D-3 is close to this line — one shown case turned into an audit of our own prose. Judge it on whether a sponsor would care, not on whether the fields fill.
- **Selection of the corpus.** Which talks were reviewed at depth, and which candidate findings never reached a card, is invisible. The count of discards is not a substitute for the reader judging them.
- **A well-written wrong read.** THE READ is self-graded. Its falsifier makes it checkable later; nothing catches it this week, and the falsifier will usually point at a test David controls.
- **The shelf going quiet.** Triggers now have watchers, but nothing forces the shelf to be re-read. Over a quarter this section will drift toward confirming plans already held unless the shelf is actively worked.
- **`Weakest link` and `Wrong if` becoming formula.** Both are the hardest slots to fill dishonestly and both are fillable lazily. If two weeks running produce "one shown case, no rate," the sections are not being read closely enough.
- **The sample-size bracket being cut for space.** The spec calls it invalid to omit, which is a rule, not a mechanism. It is still the first thing an editor deletes.
- **Six cards on a phone.** The cap holds the word budget; it does not guarantee anyone scrolls past card three. The ledger has to carry a reader who stops there.
